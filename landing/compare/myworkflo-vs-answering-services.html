Should an HVAC shop use a human answering service or AI?

The classic fix for missed calls is a human answering service; the new one is software. Neither is universally better. Humans are warmer and handle messy conversations; software is instant, doesn't bill by the minute, and never has a queue. Here's the honest comparison, using Ruby — a well-known human service with published pricing — as the benchmark.

When is a human answering service the better choice?

Pick a human service like Ruby when the voice itself is the product: high-trust customers, complex or emotional conversations, callers who won't text. If your volume is modest — a few short calls a day fits comfortably in a 100–200 minute plan — the per-minute model is affordable and every caller gets a warm, live person around the clock. That experience is genuinely hard for software to match.

When is AI the better choice?

Pick software when the problem is volume, speed, and predictability. Minutes-based pricing punishes busy seasons — a July heat wave is exactly when your answering bill spikes — while flat pricing doesn't move. Text-back also beats hold queues for scheduling: the customer answers qualifying questions on their own time and books against your real availability, and your team only gets pulled in for the calls that actually need a human. If HVAC-specific handling matters — emergency keywords, equipment questions, Spanish-first callers — that's built in rather than scripted by you.

Can you combine them?
