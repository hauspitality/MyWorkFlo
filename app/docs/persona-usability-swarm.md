# Persona Usability Swarm — MyWorkFlo

*Simulated user-testing study · 10 independent buyer personas · September 22, 2026*

## Executive Summary

Ten simulated target-customer personas — from a 3-man owner-operator to a liability-focused after-hours manager to a 90-second cold visitor — independently evaluated MyWorkFlo end to end: the live landing site, the live login surface, every onboarding and dashboard screen, and the exact SMS copy customers and staff would receive.

**Overall: 4.8/10 average, with a tight 4–6 range across all ten personas.** That consistency is the headline: the product's core is widely trusted, but every persona hit the same wall — **the product cannot currently be tried, bought, or fully believed.**

| Persona | Seat | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Ray | Owner-operator, 3-person HVAC shop | 4/10 |
| Marisol | Office manager/dispatcher, 12 techs | 5/10 |
| Dennis | After-hours emergency service manager | 5/10 |
| Cody | Field technician who helps schedule | 6/10 |
| Barbara | Non-technical owner, AI skeptic | 4/10 |
| Tanya | High-volume dispatcher | 4/10 |
| Gus | Price-sensitive one-truck contractor | 5/10 |
| Priya | Growth-focused two-location owner | 5/10 |
| Sam | Customer-service/brand-voice manager | 5/10 |
| Alex | First-time cold visitor | 5/10 |

**What's genuinely working** (near-unanimous): the emergency-safety architecture (locked scripts, bilingual detection, bypasses every control mode — 9/10 personas praised it unprompted); the Draft/Assisted/Autopilot control-mode model as an AI-trust on-ramp; the SMS-native approval spine (YES/NO replies + no-login magic links); and the AI voice rules that forbid invented prices and times.

**What's blocking adoption** (near-unanimous): the primary login button fails (Google provider unconfigured); the landing page has no path to signup at all — every CTA dead-ends at an audit form with no follow-through; nothing anywhere explains how the product connects to the buyer's existing phone number; the pricing tiers describe feature differences that don't exist in the product; the onboarding finale claims "a missed call will now get a text back" when no phone line is connected; and several landing claims (CRM integrations, urgent voice calls, estimate follow-ups, opt-out language) are not present in the shipped code.

**Verdict shared across personas: do not send real traffic yet.** The distance to "ready" is short — the top recommendations below are mostly copy, wiring, and disclosure fixes rather than new systems — but today every persona either bounced at the front door or lost trust at a promise the product couldn't keep.

## Testing Method

**Approach.** A parallel "swarm" of 10 persona agents, each given a distinct identity, job-to-be-done, fear, and exploration focus, evaluated the product independently (no shared state, no visibility into each other's findings), followed by a separate synthesis pass across all ten reports. Personas were instructed to be candid, to ground every finding in a specific screen/route/file/copy string, and to avoid reading the repo's prior audit documents so their findings would be unanchored.

**Surfaces exercised.**
- *Live, GET-only:* myworkflo.com (landing, /alternatives, /compare/*), app.myworkflo.com/login, /privacy, /terms.
- *Code-grounded walkthroughs* of every authenticated screen (onboarding steps, dashboard home/leads/lead detail/approvals/activity/settings, the dev simulator, the /a/[token] magic-link page) and of the exact SMS strings customers and staff receive (missed-call opener, AI voice rules, approval and confirmation texts, emergency scripts, expiry fallbacks).

**Limitations (themselves findings).** Authenticated flows were walked by reading route source rather than clicking, because the primary sign-in (Google) is not yet configured and no SMS number is provisioned — meaning no persona could complete a real signup or receive a real text. Where reading code was a poor substitute for using a screen, personas said so. No writes, sends, or purchases of any kind were performed.

# Persona Feedback

### Ray — Owner-operator of a 3-man HVAC shop in upstate NY who runs his business from a phone in an attic

**Who I am:** Ray, 51. Me plus two techs. My cell is the office. I miss around five calls a day because I'm elbow-deep in an air handler when they come in. The only software I pay for is QuickBooks, and I only half-trust that.

**My job-to-be-done:** Stop losing the customer who calls at 2pm while I'm in a crawlspace, without hiring a $40k office person — and without a robot saying something stupid to Mrs. Delaney, who I've serviced for eleven years.

**My biggest fear/objection:** The robot embarrasses me. It quotes a price I never agreed to, promises a time I can't make, or texts something canned to a customer who knows my voice. In this town, that costs me more than the missed call did.

**What I tried to do:** Read the landing page top to bottom on my phone (hero, "Here's what other options get wrong," the free audit form, pricing, safety section, FAQ). Clicked "Sign in" to app.myworkflo.com/login. Walked all 8 onboarding steps (business → hours → service area → appointment types → emergency keywords → control mode → calendar → plan → done). Then looked hard at what my customers and my guys would actually get by text — the missed-call opener, the emergency scripts, the "reply YES to send" approval texts, the booking confirmation, and what happens when I don't answer an approval for 15 minutes. (Honest caveat: past the login screen I was reading the product's code, not tapping real screens, because Google sign-in isn't wired up and there's no live phone number yet — so some of this is "what the screens would do," not "what they did." That gap is itself a finding: there was no way for me to actually try the thing.)

**What I understood quickly:**
- The landing page talks like a person who's met a contractor. "The customer who calls while you're driving, a tech is on a roof, or the office is closed" (section-split copy) — that's my Tuesday. "1 job can pay for the month" (numbers section) is exactly the math I'd do on a napkin.
- The three modes (Draft / Assisted / Autopilot, #modes section and the onboarding control-mode step) answered my robot fear before I asked it. Starting in Draft where I approve every text? That I'd actually do.
- The safety stance is the most convincing thing in the whole product. The locked scripts in emergencyScripts.ts ("leave the house now and call 911... Don't use light switches or your phone while inside") are exactly what I'd want said, the bilingual trigger list catches "huele a gas" and misspellings, and the onboarding copy "no waiting on approval, ever" is the right call. Someone who understands gas wrote this.
- The approval text my guys would get is genuinely usable from a truck: "MyWorkFlo drafted a reply to [number]: '...' — reply YES to send, NO to discard" (process-inbound.ts). No app, no login. That's how you build for people like me.

**What confused me:**
- **Nowhere — not the landing page, not onboarding — does anyone tell me how this connects to MY phone number.** The trust strip says "Business phone," onboarding asks for "Your cell phone" (business-form.tsx), but there is no step for my actual business line. The code (api/webhooks/twilio/voice/route.ts) shows the real answer: customers call a new Twilio number that bridges to my cell — meaning I'd forward my line or advertise a new number. That's a huge practical question for a guy whose number is on 500 fridge magnets, and the product never says it out loud.
- **Which plan am I?** Landing says Starter $119 is "solo operators" and Growth $299 is "2 to 10 tech businesses that need... office approval controls." I'm 3 guys and no office. And the bullets don't map to anything in the app — the onboarding plan step (onboarding/plan/page.tsx) is just three prices with zero feature differences, and the approval controls and dashboard that Growth "adds" looked available to everyone in the code. Does Starter's "Sends leads by email or sheet" mean I lose the dashboard at $119? Nobody says.
- **The appointment-types step is where I'd stall.** Duration in minutes, "hvac_issue_codes" checkboxes, a nested pricing panel with "is_quotable_by_ai" (appointment-types-editor.tsx) — that's the first screen that smells like software built for software people. Worse: each row has its own Save button, but the big blue Continue navigates away whether or not I saved (appointment-types-step.tsx just does router.push) — I would absolutely edit a duration, hit Continue, and silently lose it.
- Service area is ZIP codes only (service-area-editor.tsx, `type: "zip_list"`). I cover half a county up here. That's thirty ZIPs typed into a textarea on a phone.

**Where I lost trust:**
- **The finish line lies to me.** The done page says "A missed call will now get a text back automatically" (onboarding/done/page.tsx) — but no phone number was ever provisioned or connected, so nothing of the sort will happen. The calendar and billing steps earned trust by being honest about not being configured; the done page spends it.
- **The front door is broken.** "Continue with Google — no password to remember" is the primary login button, and Google auth isn't configured — so my very first tap on this product would fail with "Something went wrong. Try again in a moment" (login/page.tsx). For a guy already suspicious of SaaS, that's often the last tap too.
- **The landing page promises things the texts don't do.** The "Texts back missed calls" card says "Includes the required opt-out text" — the actual opener in missed-call.ts ("Sorry we missed your call — this is {business}. Text back what's going on...") has no opt-out language at all. The hero says it "calls you if it sounds urgent" — the emergency path in process-inbound.ts sends me an SMS and a push, it never calls anyone. Small gaps, but they're exactly the "says one thing, does another" pattern I'm afraid of from the robot itself.
- **15 minutes.** Approvals expire in 15 minutes (migration 0001: `now() + interval '15 minutes'`; confirmed by the magic-link page copy). I'm in attics with one bar of signal. Most of my approvals would expire, my customer gets "someone from our team will text you back shortly" (expire-approvals/route.ts), and now the robot has written a check that I have to cash — the exact embarrassment I bought Draft mode to avoid. There's no setting to change it (settings-client.tsx has sections for hours/area/types/keywords/staff/calendar/billing/account — nothing for this).

**What felt valuable:** The locked, non-editable safety scripts and the bright-line "person instructions yes, equipment instructions never" policy. Draft mode as a starting posture. YES/NO approval by plain text with a no-login magic link (a/[token]/page.tsx). The AI voice rules in systemPrompt.ts — "never state a dollar figure that didn't come from a tool," "come take a look" instead of "diagnostic" — read like they were written by someone who's heard a bad answering service. The turn cap that forces a human after ~8 exchanges. The booking confirmation "You're all set — we'll be out Fri, Sep 26 at 10:00 AM. Reply here if anything changes" (resolve.ts) is fine, though it breaks the product's own "spell out day names, drop :00" voice rule.

**What felt unnecessary:** The "Free Missed Call & Booking Audit" form (index.html #audit) — every button on the pricing cards routes me there instead of to sign-up, and it outputs a canned paragraph, not my numbers. "Form 04822 / Work order intake" theming is cute but it's a hoop. The SMS simulator card sitting on my main dashboard under a /dashboard/dev/ route with a "seed dev business" button (dashboard/page.tsx, simulator-client.tsx) — the idea is actually great for my fear (let me watch the robot before customers do), but it's dressed as developer test gear, not "Try it safely." PushDemo on the home screen likewise.

**What would make me convert/stay:** (1) A working front door and a real phone path — the day I can call a number, hang up, and watch my own phone get the text back, I'll believe all of it. (2) A "connect your phone line" step with forwarding instructions in plain English, before the done page claims it's live. (3) An approval window I can set to an hour, because attics. Do those and the safety design already on the page would keep me.

**Rating: 4/10** — The thinking is genuinely right for a guy like me (Draft mode, locked safety scripts, YES/NO by text), but today the primary login button fails, no phone line can be connected, and the setup flow ends by telling me something works that doesn't — I can't run my business on a promissory note.

**My top 3 asks:** 1. Wire the core loop before the copy claims it: provision the number, add a "connect/forward your business line" onboarding step, and make the done page tell the truth until then. 2. Fix the first-tap failures and silent losses: configure Google sign-in (or demote the button), and make the appointment-types Continue save or warn about unsaved rows. 3. Make approvals fit a tradesman's day: configurable expiry (15 min default is office-worker time), and make the landing's plan tiers match what the app actually gates — tell me plainly which plan a 3-man shop buys and what I'd give up at $119.

### Marisol — Office manager/dispatcher at a 12-tech home-service company, the one who'd babysit this thing

**Who I am:** I run the front desk 7–6. Phones, dispatch board, angry customers, techs texting me from attics. The owner asked me to evaluate MyWorkFlo because I'm the person who'd live in it — and the person who gets blamed when a tool "saves time" by creating a second inbox I have to watch.

**My job-to-be-done:** When a call slips past me (I'm on the other line 40 times a day), I need the system to catch it, and then I need one screen that tells me — in under 5 seconds — WHO is waiting, WHAT they want, and WHAT happens if I tap Approve. Then I need to get back to my real phone.

**My biggest fear/objection:** Another dashboard that pages me constantly but makes me click three times to find out what it's even asking about. Approval fatigue is how these tools die at my desk — I start rubber-stamping, and then one day I rubber-stamp a booking for a Saturday we don't work.

**What I tried to do:** Read myworkflo.com top to bottom (hero, modes, pricing, safety FAQ, /alternatives, both /compare pages — all load, all live). Hit app.myworkflo.com/login. Then, since there's no real Twilio number provisioned yet, I walked the code for every screen I'd use: `dashboard/page.tsx` (home), `dashboard/leads/page.tsx` + `leads/[id]/page.tsx` (list + thread), `dashboard/approvals/page.tsx` + `approval-card.tsx` (my queue), the no-login SMS approval page `a/[token]/page.tsx`, `dashboard/activity/page.tsx`, `dashboard/settings/settings-client.tsx`, and the actual SMS strings in `lib/twilio/missed-call.ts`, `lib/messaging/process-inbound.ts`, `lib/hvac/emergencyScripts.ts`, `lib/approvals/resolve.ts`, and the expiry cron. **Honest caveat: reading code is not clicking.** I couldn't feel approval latency, see the queue with 15 real items, or experience the actual missed-call → text loop, because no Twilio number exists yet. That's itself a finding: nobody can trial the core product loop today except through the built-in SMS simulator.

**What I understood quickly:**
- The home dashboard is triage-first and that's correct: "Need your attention" is the first stat, turns red when nonzero, the bell icon carries a pending-count badge (`dashboard-shell.tsx` NotificationBell), and the needs-attention list is oldest-first with color-coded urgency rails. Lead statuses are plain-English chips — "Awaiting approval," "Emergency," "Booked" — not codes.
- Draft / Assisted / Autopilot is explained in my language, and the line "safety emergencies always send an instant, pre-approved reply... this setting never affects that" (`control-mode-selector.tsx`) is exactly the promise I'd want in writing.
- The emergency handling is the most trustworthy part of the whole product. Locked scripts that only ever direct the PERSON (leave, call 911), never the equipment (`emergencyScripts.ts` bright-line policy), Spanish trigger phrases ("huele a gas") in `emergencySignals.ts`, the AI going silent after escalation instead of "continuing to help," and a staff text that says "Call them now: [number]." I'd defend that design to my owner.
- Expired approvals don't strand the customer: after 15 minutes the cron texts them "someone from our team will text you back shortly," escalates the conversation, and sends staff "that someone is you" (`expire-approvals/route.ts`). The system nags me, but it never leaves a homeowner on read. Good.
- The customer-facing texts are genuinely how I'd write them: "Sorry we missed your call — this is {business}. Text back what's going on and we'll get you taken care of" (`missed-call.ts`), and "You're all set — we'll be out Tue, Sep 23, 10:00 AM" in our timezone (`resolve.ts`). The AI voice rules (`systemPrompt.ts` — no jargon, no invented prices or times, 3–4 messages max) read like they interviewed someone who does my job.

**What confused me:** (specific, cited)
- **Approval cards don't say WHO.** `approvals/page.tsx` builds the card summary as `draft_text ?? reason ?? holding_text`. For a **booking** approval, the payload is only `{appointment_type_id, start}` (`process-inbound.ts`, queue_booking_approval), so my card literally reads: "Booking needs approval — See conversation for details." No customer name. No phone. No date or time. No job type. And **the card has no link to the conversation** — `approval-card.tsx` renders type label, summary, expiry, Approve/Decline, and nothing else. I would be confirming an appointment without knowing who it's for or when it is.
- The absurd part: the SMS magic-link page **does it right** — `a/[token]/page.tsx` `buildSummary()` looks up the appointment type name and formats the start time in our timezone ("Diagnostic — Tue, Sep 23, 10:00 AM"). So the throwaway page a tech opens from a text shows more context than the queue at my desk. Backwards.
- Two pending "Message needs approval" cards from different customers are indistinguishable except by timestamp — same problem, message flavor.
- Dashboard "Upcoming appointments" stat links to `/dashboard/leads` (`dashboard/page.tsx` stats array), not to any schedule view. Small thing, wrong door.
- Leads list has search but **no status filter** (`leads/page.tsx` filters only name/phone/issue against the last 50 conversations). I can't pull up "show me everything escalated" — the exact list I'd work first thing every morning.
- Thread bubbles print raw internals — "ai · sent," "customer · delivered" in uppercase (`leads/[id]/page.tsx`). Reads like a debug view. (The "draft — awaiting approval" tag is good though.)
- Activity is an honest log but a dead end: humanized event names plus raw metadata pairs like "appointment_type_id: 3f2c…" (`activity/page.tsx` metadataSummary) and **no link from any event to its lead or conversation.** It proves "every call is logged" but I can't act from it.

**Where I lost trust:** (specific, cited)
- **I can't talk to my own customer inside the product.** The lead thread has Call/Text buttons that are `tel:`/`sms:` links (`leads/[id]/page.tsx`) — meaning I'd text from MY personal cell, from a different number than the business line the customer has been texting, and my reply would never appear in the thread. There is no compose box, no "I've got this" takeover, no close-conversation action anywhere (I grepped — nothing). So every "needs a human" escalation ends with the product shrugging me out the door, and the thread record silently goes incomplete the moment a human does their job.
- **There is one seat, and it isn't mine.** `settings-client.tsx` Staff section: "Inviting additional staff is coming soon." The owner signs up with his Google account and his cell (`onboarding/business` asks for "Your cell phone"), and `notify-staff.ts` texts "every active staff phone" — which is just him. Every approval, every emergency, every URGENT expired-approval nudge goes to the owner's pocket, not the front desk. The Growth plan is sold as "office approval controls" for 2–10 tech shops — but the office can't have a login.
- **The 15-minute approval expiry is invisible and unconfigurable.** The only place it's stated is the magic-link expired page ("Approval requests expire 15 minutes after they're sent," `a/[token]/page.tsx`). Nothing in Settings shows or changes it. In Draft mode at real volume, my lunch break converts the queue into a pile of auto-escalations plus urgent texts. The dashboard card does show a live "Expires in 12m" countdown (`approval-card.tsx`) — good — but I never agreed to the 15.
- **The landing page promises things the app doesn't do.** Hero and FAQ say it "calls you if it sounds urgent" — the code sends SMS + push (`notify-staff.ts`); nobody gets called. The trust strip lists Jobber and Housecall Pro, Pro tier says "Connects to your CRM," Growth says "Follows up on estimates," Starter says "Sends leads by email or sheet" — I found none of that in the app. When my owner asks "where's the Jobber sync," I'm the one holding the bag.
- **First 30 seconds are broken.** The login page's one visible button is "Continue with Google" ("One tap with Google — no password to remember," `login/page.tsx`) — and Google isn't configured on the live deployment, so it errors, and I'd have to notice the small grey "Or get a sign-in link by email" text link. A broken primary button on screen one is exactly how I'd expect the rest to go — even though, having read the code, the rest is actually better than that.
- **I can't buy it even if I wanted to.** Every landing CTA — including "Start with Growth" on the $299 card — goes to `#audit`, a canned calculator. The only route into the app is the small "Sign in" link. No signup path from the marketing site at all.

**What felt valuable:** The missed-call text-back opener; the locked emergency scripts and the fact the AI shuts up after escalating; approve-by-SMS with YES/NO plus a magic link that needs no login (perfect for an owner on a roof); the expiry safety net that never ghosts the customer; oldest-first queue ordering; plain-English statuses; the control-mode dial with the safety carve-out; honest "not configured" copy everywhere (calendar: "the AI offers simulated availability... Autopilot bookings fall back to staff approval"; plan: "Billing isn't live yet — you're on the house for now"). And invisible things I only know from reading code but would have felt as reliability: webhook dedup so customers never get double-texted, and Autopilot re-verifying a slot is actually open before booking it (`process-inbound.ts`).

**What felt unnecessary:** The "SMS simulator" and push-notification demo cards taking up half the quick-actions area on the production home dashboard (`dashboard/page.tsx` — dev tooling in my face every morning); the landing's skeuomorphic "Work order intake / Form 04822" audit widget whose "result" is essentially the same canned recommendation regardless; a Pro tier ($599) selling routing rules and CRM integrations that don't exist yet.

**What would make me convert/stay:** Show me who/what/when on every approval card with a one-tap link into the thread — the code to build that summary already exists on the token page, it just never made it to my queue. Let me answer a customer from inside the app, from the business number, with the reply recorded in the thread. Give me my own seat with my own phone number for alerts, and let the owner route after-hours to on-call instead of everything going to one cell. Do those three and this goes from "another thing to babysit" to "the first tab I open" — because the bones (safety discipline, honest statuses, the escalation safety net) are genuinely better than the big-platform tools we've demoed.

**Rating: 5/10** — The triage surfaces and safety design are real and I'd trust the texts it sends, but today I'd be approving bookings blind from a context-free card, I can't reply to a customer in-app, and the product only has one seat — the owner's — so the person the Growth plan is sold to (me) can't actually use it.

**My top 3 asks:** 1. Context on every approval: customer name + phone, job type, and formatted date/time on the card (reuse `a/[token]` `buildSummary()`), plus a link to the conversation — in the queue, on the dashboard needs-attention list, and in the staff SMS. 2. In-app reply/takeover from the lead thread that sends from the business Twilio number and lands in the thread, plus a "mark handled/close" action so escalations can actually end inside the product. 3. Staff invites with per-person notification routing (front desk during business hours, on-call after) — and until multi-seat ships, stop selling Growth as "office approval controls."

### Dennis — After-hours/emergency service manager for a 24/7 HVAC company, liability-obsessed

**Who I am:** I run the overnight side of a shop with a real 24/7 line and an on-call rotation. When something goes wrong at 2am, my name is on the incident report. I've fired answering services for less.

**My job-to-be-done:** Cover the overnight window without a human dispatcher — but ONLY if I can prove to myself (and to a lawyer, later) that a gas leak, CO event, or electrical fire gets the right words to the customer and a human on my team actually woken up, every single time.

**My biggest fear/objection:** The AI freelances safety advice at 2am ("just shut off the gas valve"), or tells a customer "we've alerted the team" when nobody's phone actually rang.

**What I tried to do:** Read the landing page (myworkflo.com, the "Careful by design" `#safety` section and FAQ), then went straight at the emergency machinery: `app/lib/hvac/emergencyScripts.ts` and `emergencySignals.ts` (+ its test file), the AI constitution in `app/lib/ai/systemPrompt.ts`, the two-layer flag path in `lib/ai/engine.ts` and `lib/ai/tools.ts`, the enforcement order in `lib/ai/decide-action.ts` + `outputGuardrails.ts`, then the full escalation path in `lib/messaging/process-inbound.ts` → `notify-staff.ts`, the missed-call opener in `lib/twilio/missed-call.ts`, approvals in `lib/approvals/resolve.ts`, the 15-minute expiry sweeper (`app/api/cron/expire-approvals/route.ts`), the `/a/[token]` no-login approval page, and the onboarding emergency-keywords/control-mode screens and Settings. Important caveat: I could only READ the 2am flow, not live-fire it — Google sign-in isn't configured and there's no Twilio number yet, so I never actually received the staff SMS or push on my own phone. For an emergency product, "trust me, the code sends it" is exactly the kind of evidence I can't take to an insurance adjuster.

**What I understood quickly:** The safety architecture is written by someone who thinks like me. `emergencyScripts.ts` has an explicit bright-line policy — person-directed instructions only (leave, call 911), never equipment-directed ("even 'just shut off the gas valve'") — locked, platform-authored, not business-editable, not model-generated. Layer 1 detection (`emergencySignals.ts`) is deterministic, runs before any LLM call, is deliberately biased to over-trigger, checks English AND Spanish on every message, and even catches the CO case where nobody says "carbon monoxide" (furnace + dizzy/headache co-occurrence). Emergencies bypass control mode entirely and skip the debounce (`process-inbound.ts` line ~207: "Layer-1 emergencies never wait"). The `flag_emergency` tool short-circuits to the locked script — the model's freeform text is never used in an emergency. `decideAction`'s enforcement order (emergency > guardrails > control mode) lives in code, not prompt. And after escalation the thread goes dead-simple: any further text gets one fixed acknowledgment, no more AI "helping." That restraint is rare and correct.

**What confused me:**
- The onboarding "Safety triggers" screen (`onboarding/emergency-keywords/page.tsx`) shows five categories with two examples each and says "no waiting on approval, ever" — but never shows me the actual safety script my customers will receive, or lets me test-fire it from that screen. I'd want to read the exact gas-smell text before I bet my license on it.
- `Control mode` copy says emergencies "notify you immediately" — but nothing anywhere tells me HOW (SMS? push? call?), what happens if that notification fails, or how to configure who gets it at 2am vs 2pm.
- The `flag_emergency` tool schema demands an `evidence_quote` "for the audit log" (`lib/ai/tools.ts` line ~108) — but `engine.ts` discards it, and the `emergency_escalated` audit row only records `{ category }` (`process-inbound.ts` line ~285). The Layer-1 `matchedPhrase` is dropped too. My incident report needs "what exact words triggered this," and the audit trail promises it and doesn't deliver it.

**Where I lost trust:**
1. **The staff alert is one best-effort SMS + one push, fired once, with no acknowledgment loop.** `notify-staff.ts` is explicitly "best-effort... never throws"; failures go to `audit_log` (`staff_sms_failed` / `staff_push_failed`) that nobody is watching at 2am. There is no retry, no re-page after N minutes of silence, no voice call (the one channel that defeats Do Not Disturb), and no "a human acknowledged this" state. Meanwhile the locked script has already told the customer "We've alerted [business]'s team right now" — the code comment at `process-inbound.ts` line ~287 even says "make that true," but a single failed SMS makes it false with no recovery. The 15-minute expiry sweeper re-nudges unanswered APPROVALS, but an emergency creates no approval row — so an emergency that nobody sees is never re-raised. This is the exact scenario on my incident report.
2. **One staff member, period.** Settings says "Inviting additional staff is coming soon" (`settings-client.tsx` line 141). So the 2am gas-leak alert goes to whoever signed up — there is no on-call rotation, no second phone, no escalation chain. For a 24/7 team this isn't a missing feature, it's the whole product.
3. **Spanish-speaking customers in a gas emergency get English safety instructions.** Detection is proudly bilingual ("huele a gas" triggers), the landing sells "English and Spanish intake," the constitution says match the customer's language — but every locked script in `emergencyScripts.ts` and the follow-up acknowledgment exist only in English. "Please leave the house now and call 911" delivered in a language the panicking customer may not read is a safety AND liability hole.
4. **Layer 1 has no FIRE/SMOKE category.** "My furnace is on fire," "the house is full of smoke," "I smell smoke" match nothing in `emergencySignals.ts` (only "smoke coming from" under sparking). Bare "carbon monoxide" without "detector/alarm" also misses. All of these fall through to Layer 2 — the model's judgment — which is precisely the layer the deterministic list exists to not depend on.
5. **Emergency threads can never be stood down.** Nothing in the codebase moves a conversation out of `escalated_emergency` (grep confirms: only `process-inbound.ts` sets it; `resolve.ts` only reactivates via approval rows, which emergencies don't create; no close/resolve action exists in `dashboard/actions.ts` or the lead detail page). ## Trust and AI Concerns

**What built trust:** The locked, platform-authored emergency scripts and the person-vs-equipment bright line (cited by 9/10); guardrails enforced in code post-generation, not prompt-wishes (Sam, Dennis); Autopilot re-verifying availability and never saying "you're booked" unless the booking saved (Priya, Barbara, Sam); the 8-turn cap forcing a human (Ray, Gus); the expiry fallback that never leaves a homeowner on read (Marisol, Priya); honest "not configured" admissions in calendar/billing settings; the /alternatives page recommending competitors (Barbara: "earned more trust than the whole homepage").

**What broke it:** The done-page claiming a dead loop is live (Ray, Gus, Sam, Priya — the single most-cited trust breach); the broken Google button as first impression; hero claims the code contradicts ("answer every call," "calls you if urgent," opt-out text — personas explicitly pattern-matched this to their fear of an AI that "says one thing, does another," Ray's words); **English-only safety scripts behind proudly bilingual detection** — Dennis called a Spanish speaker getting English evacuation instructions "a safety AND liability hole," Sam called it "the Facebook screenshot I'm afraid of, in the highest-stakes moment"; the fire-once, best-effort, single-recipient emergency alert with no acknowledgment loop while the customer is told "we've alerted the team" (Dennis: "the exact scenario on my incident report"); no AI disclosure to customers and no way to name the assistant (Barbara: "people felt tricked when they realized"); the `evidence_quote` demanded "for the audit log" then discarded (Dennis); invented "typical week" hero stats (Sam, Alex).

**Autopilot-comfort spectrum:** Barbara and Ray sit at the cautious end — Draft-only, and even then Barbara demands edit rights ("send words that aren't mine, or decline" is not control). Sam is with them: approval-without-editing isn't brand control. Cody trusts the SMS spine but not in-app blind approvals. Gus and Tanya land in the middle: both concluded Assisted is the only workable mode (Gus: Draft is "my voicemail with extra steps"; Tanya: "the queue shrinks to bookings only") — but nothing in onboarding steers buyers there. Priya is the most Autopilot-ready, precisely because of engineering: slot re-verification and the failed-booking downgrade are "what would let me ever trust Autopilot." 5. **Dev tooling on the production home** — `/dashboard` quick-actions: "SMS simulator" (labeled "Dev-only," dumps raw JSON) and PushDemo cards. Flagged by 8 personas; doubly costly because the simulator is also the most-wanted trust feature.

10. **Small but repeated:** "Upcoming appointments" stat links to leads, not the schedule (Marisol, Priya); mobile schedule card hides customer name (`hidden sm:block`), shows no address, rows not tappable (Cody); the mode chip is hidden on mobile so Barbara can't see which mode she's in; a late YES after expiry drops the tech into the customer pipeline and the AI answers him like a homeowner (Cody); YES/NO is disabled whenever more than one approval is pending, which at Tanya's volume is always.

## Suggested Next Experiments

1. **Live-loop smoke test:** provision one real number, have 5 contractors call it, hang up, and watch their phone. Metric: % who receive the text-back within 60 seconds + verbatim reaction. Success validates the activation flow all 10 personas couldn't run; any failure found here is found before a paying customer finds it. (Ray: "the day I can call a number, hang up, and watch my own phone get the text back, I'll believe all of it.")
2. **Hero honesty A/B:** "Answer every call" vs. "Text back every missed call in seconds." Metric: landing→login click-through and bounce rate. If the honest variant converts equal or better, the overclaim (flagged by Barbara, Alex, Ray) is pure downside and gets deleted.
3. **CTA rerouting test:** point pricing-card buttons at /login instead of #audit for half of traffic. Metric: accounts created per landing visit. A lift confirms the audit form is the funnel leak Barbara and Alex described; no lift means the audit is doing qualification work worth keeping.
4. **Public "Test the AI" demo:** expose the simulator (pre-loaded gas-smell and no-cooling scenarios) on the landing page, no login. Metric: % of visitors who run a conversation → % of those who sign up, vs. non-runners. Tests Barbara's claim ("if you showed me this behavior in the simulator on day one, you'd have me") and Gus's demand to test-drive before $119.
5. **Draft-mode expiry field trial:** give 3 owner-operators Draft mode for one week of real (or simulated-inbound) volume. Metric: % of approvals that expire and hit the customer-fallback path. If >30–50% expire, the 15-minute fuse is confirmed as a churn mechanism (Ray, Gus, Tanya) and expiry/mode-steering work jumps the queue.
6. **2am alert reliability drill:** fire the real emergency notification path to 5 real phones (DND on, overnight) for two weeks. Metrics: delivery rate, time-to-first-human-acknowledgment, silent failures. This produces the "acknowledged by Dennis at 2:07am" evidence Dennis says he needs for his insurance carrier — or proves the SMS+push-only design is insufficient before a real gas leak does.
7. **Approval-card context usability test:** current card vs. an enriched prototype (name, phone, job type, local time, thread link) with 5 dispatchers processing 10 mixed approvals each. Metrics: time-to-decision and wrong-approval rate. Quantifies the blind-approval risk Marisol, Cody, and Tanya all called their nightmare, and justifies (or kills) recommendation UX-3 with data.
8. **Spanish mystery shop:** have 3 Spanish speakers run the "huele a gas" flow end-to-end. Metrics: did they understand the English safety script; would they have evacuated. Any comprehension failure converts Dennis's and Sam's liability argument from hypothetical to documented, and sets the priority for Spanish script translation ahead of new features.
